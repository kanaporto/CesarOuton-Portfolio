# Milano Cortina 2026 — executive project summary

**BizTech IT readiness · Multi-site service delivery · Support-team leadership**  
*Portfolio case study · Employer anonymised · First-person account*

## Project at a glance

- **My remit:** Led the overall BizTech IT readiness and service-delivery project and directly managed the support team.
- **Cross-functional role:** Served as the coordination point across AV, Network, Infrastructure and partner teams, aligning requirements, schedules, dependencies and handoffs. Each specialist team owned its own technical tasks and implementation.
- **Scale:** Planned over approximately eight months; supported more than 200 people across three event locations; spent approximately 40 days on site in Milan. These figures are based on my first-hand account.
- **Operating footprint:** Dublin customer-support site handling event-driven demand; Milan office hub for rotating teams; and a fan zone used for public access and private partner events.

## The highest-impact work

- **Designed a remote-support-first service model** with targeted on-site coverage. I managed support-team roles, site assignments, working-day coverage and rotations, and adjusted priorities as demand changed.
- **Kept service capacity flexible** for unpredictable and late requests rather than fully committing available technician time in advance. During quieter periods at the event sites, technicians could also progress improvements at a separate, low-visit Milan office, including three video-conferencing rooms left incomplete by a parallel project.
- **Recovered venue readiness:** Site visits revealed that the historic marble fan zone lacked event-suitable infrastructure. I coordinated the service and AV planning, including placement of Starlink and a second fibre service, with the relevant specialist teams. The Milan office hub also had outstanding setup work one week before planned use, prompting additional cross-functional readiness work.
- **Delivered an urgent LA28 presentation setup:** The request arrived overnight. From 8 a.m., I worked on site with the local team to design the AV and sound solution for a venue without suitable screens or monitors, set the technical direction, and coordinate equipment movement, installation, testing and user guidance within eight hours. I remained through the presentation and organised rapid teardown before the public space reopened.
- **Supported a CFO all-hands and global stream:** Coordinated an AV/network setup for people attending in the fan zone and employees watching globally, adapting the planned solution to late changes and public-access constraints.
- **Solved a cross-border logistics challenge:** Chose a consolidated, insured removal-company truck transported by ferry from Dublin to Milan instead of multiple individual air parcels. This was a cost-conscious choice; savings were not quantified.
- **Reported and improved operations:** Sent daily status reports to stakeholders, including global IT leadership, and turned fixes into reusable runbooks, as corroborated by a peer recommendation.

## Outcomes and evidence

- My former employer’s performance review recognised the support plan, hardware procurement, supplier onboarding, training materials and cross-functional coordination. It described operations as seamless, without service interruptions or delays; this is an attributed assessment, not independently audited uptime.
- Jeff Galisky’s peer recommendation corroborated continuity, fallback planning, delivery reliability and reusable runbooks. [View my LinkedIn profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cesarouton/).
- The wider event was described to me as a multimillion-euro investment. This was not the IT budget, and I do not claim ownership of the total event spend.

## Technology and method

Neat room systems · Zoom Rooms · Cisco video-conferencing · endpoint devices · network dependencies · AV and streaming coordination.

I organise the work into six project phases and map them retrospectively to ITIL Version 5 concepts: **Discover** (sites, users and constraints) → **Design and Plan** (service model, team, coverage and contingencies) → **Deliver and Validate** (procurement, logistics, readiness and handover) → **Operate and Support** (live service, triage, change and reporting) → **Decommission and Close** (recovery and handover) → **Continual Improvement** (lessons and reusable runbooks). The four dimensions—Organisations and people, Value streams and processes, Information and technology, and Partners and suppliers—are woven through the lifecycle. This is a retrospective framework for explaining my work, not a claim that the organisation formally adopted ITIL.
